# Asset-Pricing Section Blueprints

## Abstract

Use: economic object → universe/approach → central magnitude → benchmark/validation → interpretation and limit. State whether evidence is in sample, out of sample, or postpublication.

## Introduction

1. Establish the pricing or investment puzzle.
2. Explain why it matters for a model, market efficiency, or investor decision.
3. Identify why existing evidence cannot distinguish explanations.
4. Describe the asset universe, signal/model, timing, and test.
5. Give the headline spread/pricing error/forecast gain with units.
6. Present decisive validation and implementation evidence.
7. Compare with the closest return fact/model, not the whole asset-pricing literature.
8. State whether the paper changes a fact, model assessment, mechanism, or feasible strategy.

## Data and construction

- database vintages and availability;
- security filters, share codes, exchanges, delistings;
- signal definition and lag;
- breakpoints, weights, rebalance frequency;
- return horizon and overlap;
- factor/benchmark source and construction;
- missing data and extreme observations;
- train/validation/test split when relevant.

## Results

Recommended evidence order:

1. Economic shape and baseline magnitude.
2. Benchmark-adjusted result/model fit.
3. Stability and influential segments.
4. Model-specific or mechanism predictions.
5. Holdout, post-discovery, or external validation.
6. Costs, constraints, and feasibility.

## Conclusion

State what is learned about expected returns/model performance, the most decisive validation, and the boundary imposed by sample, search, or implementation. Do not convert prediction into causality or model rejection into proof of an alternative.
