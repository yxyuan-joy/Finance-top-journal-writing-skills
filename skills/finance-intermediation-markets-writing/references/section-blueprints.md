# Intermediation and Markets Section Blueprints

## Abstract

Institution/friction → data/design or model → main behavioral margin and magnitude → price/quantity/incidence consequence → mechanism and equilibrium boundary.

## Introduction

1. Institutional tension and why it matters.
2. Why equilibrium data make the mechanism hard to identify.
3. Setting/data and source of leverage.
4. Institutional outcome and main magnitude.
5. Evidence distinguishing supply/demand, information/inventory, or exposure/contagion.
6. Substitution, real effects, or aggregate consequences.
7. Contribution on the exact institutional margin.

## Setting

Explain agents, contracts/venue, cash/security/data flows, timing, information, constraints, and regulatory authority. Use a diagram if readers otherwise must hold more than two bilateral relationships in mind.

## Data

- level and frequency;
- entity/transaction matching;
- time zones and timestamp precision;
- application/order/quote/trade inclusion;
- balance-sheet reporting lag;
- cancellation, censoring, and missingness;
- aggregation and weighting;
- external representativeness.

## Results

1. Validate exposure/constraint/information measure.
2. Show behavior at the relevant institutional margin.
3. Separate competing sides/channels.
4. Report price and quantity/incidence.
5. Establish real, informational, or stability consequence.
6. Test substitution, spillovers, and aggregate response.

## Conclusion

State what the institution/mechanism changes, who bears the incidence, and what remains local or equilibrium-dependent. Keep welfare language conditional unless explicitly quantified.
