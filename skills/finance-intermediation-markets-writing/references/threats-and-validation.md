# Institutional Threats and Validation

## Credit and intermediation

| Threat | Evidence to consider |
|---|---|
| Demand versus supply | applications, within-borrower multiple lenders, commitments/drawdowns, price/nonprice terms |
| Borrower selection | observables before approval, rejected applicants, within-risk cells, composition changes |
| Lender matching | relationship history, geographic/product market, lender×borrower effects, assignment process |
| Balance-sheet endogeneity | predetermined shock/exposure, timing, asset/liability revaluation, common local shocks |
| Substitution | other lenders/products/nonbanks, aggregate borrower credit, extensive margin |
| Real effects | timing, alternative finance, downstream investment/employment/default |

## Market structure

| Threat | Evidence to consider |
|---|---|
| Mechanical bid-ask relation | effective/realized spreads, price impact, benchmark timing |
| Information versus inventory | order-flow signs, subsequent returns, dealer inventory, public news controls |
| Selection into venue/order type | routing rules, instrument/client effects, quasi-random assignment |
| Timestamp errors | clock synchronization, latency, reporting delay, trade/quote matching windows |
| Anticipation/adaptation | pre-period behavior, phased implementation, strategic order redesign |
| Market-wide substitution | other venues, internalization, dark trading, cross-asset response |

## Fragility and networks

- Separate common shocks from propagation.
- Measure direction, netting, maturity, collateral, and seniority of links.
- Validate exposures before the shock.
- Distinguish mark-to-market loss, realized loss, withdrawal, and forced sale.
- Show amplification beyond direct exposure.
- Discuss missing links and network censoring.

## Writing rule

For each validation, state the institutional alternative it targets. Avoid generic `robust to controls` language.
