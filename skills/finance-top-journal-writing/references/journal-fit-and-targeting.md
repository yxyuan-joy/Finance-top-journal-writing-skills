# Journal Fit and Targeting

## Separate fit from formatting

A journal adapter can reproduce recent heading and production conventions; it cannot infer editorial demand or acceptance probability. Target-journal advice is a provisional argument about contribution fit, not a promise that a paper "sounds like" JF, JFE, or RFS.

## Build a fit record

Compare candidate journals on the same paper facts:

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Economic object | Is the central object clearly within the journal's current official scope? |
| Contribution type | Is the advance a new fact, identification result, model, measurement tool, synthesis, or policy counterfactual? |
| Audience breadth | Who beyond the immediate setting or method needs the answer, and why? |
| Closest papers | Which verified recent papers are closest in question and contribution type, not merely topic? |
| Evidence completeness | Does the current design/model support the headline claim without promised future analysis? |
| Exposition fit | Can the paper make its question and result legible to the journal's broad finance readership? |
| Live constraints | Do current official scope, submission, anonymity, data/code, and format rules create a material constraint? |

Keep facts and claim strength fixed while comparing journals. Do not strengthen the contribution, add a causal verb, or invent a literature gap to make one target appear feasible.

## Deliver a bounded recommendation

When asked to choose a target, report:

1. a provisional first choice and at most one serious alternative;
2. the two or three paper-specific reasons that drive the ranking;
3. confidence (`low`, `moderate`, or `high`);
4. the missing evidence or editorial information most likely to reverse the choice;
5. presentation changes that do not alter the scientific claim.

Use low confidence when the closest literature has not been verified, the manuscript is incomplete, or the choice rests on assumed editorial taste. Official aims and submission pages support scope and compliance statements; published-paper patterns support only observed architecture. Neither establishes a hidden quality threshold, topic demand, or probability of acceptance.
