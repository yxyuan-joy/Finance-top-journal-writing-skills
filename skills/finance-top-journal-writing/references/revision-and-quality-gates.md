# Revision and Quality Gates

## Diagnose before editing

Rank issues in this order:

1. Unsupported or overstated claim.
2. Broken question–design–evidence chain.
3. Inconsistent numbers, samples, definitions, or signs.
4. Missing alternative explanation or design threat.
5. Section architecture and paragraph order.
6. Sentence clarity, concision, and grammar.
7. Formatting.

Do not spend the revision budget polishing sentences that support the wrong claim.

## Eight quality dimensions

Score each 0–2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Evidence fidelity | invented/conflicting | partly traceable | fully traceable |
| Argument architecture | broken | recoverable | complete chain |
| Design specificity | label only | partial | estimand, variation, assumptions, threats |
| Claim calibration | overclaimed | mixed | matched to evidence |
| Citation integrity | invented/misused | unverified gaps | verified and exact |
| Exhibit integration | disconnected | referenced | each exhibit serves a claim |
| Clarity | opaque/repetitive | uneven | precise and economical |
| Journal adaptation | stereotype/compliance guess | generic | conservative and sourced |

Any fabricated fact/citation or unjustified causal upgrade is a hard failure regardless of total score.

## Cross-section checks

Compare abstract, introduction, methods, results, conclusion, tables, and appendix for:

- main N and date window;
- unit of observation;
- treatment/predictor and outcome definitions;
- coefficient sign, scale, units, and benchmark;
- preferred specification;
- confidence interval/standard error language;
- causal/predictive/descriptive wording;
- mechanism status;
- contribution count and wording.

## Line-editing rules

- Preserve technical terms that carry meaning.
- Replace vague referents (`this`, `it`, `these results`) when ambiguity is possible.
- Prefer active voice when the actor matters; passive voice is acceptable for procedures.
- Remove stacked nouns and empty evaluative adjectives.
- Keep parallel structure in lists and contribution statements.
- Split sentences that combine design, result, mechanism, and implication.
- Retain author voice; do not force a uniform rhythm.

## Audit output

For each material issue, report:

```text
Priority: fatal / major / moderate / polish
Location:
Claim or text:
Problem:
Why it matters:
Evidence needed or revision strategy:
Suggested wording (only if supported):
```

Separate diagnosis from optional rewritten text so the author can evaluate the substantive choice.
