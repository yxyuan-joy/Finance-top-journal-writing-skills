# Title and Abstract

## Title

Choose among three defensible forms:

1. **Phenomenon**: name the economic object or puzzle.
2. **Relationship + setting**: name the variables and informative context.
3. **Question or contrast**: use only when it sharpens the tension rather than adding drama.

Prefer a title that is specific enough to distinguish the paper but broad enough to match the actual evidence. Add the design (`Evidence from ...`) only when the setting itself conveys identification or scope. Do not put `causal`, `effect`, `impact`, or a policy conclusion in the title unless supported.

Title audit:

- Does it name the central economic object?
- Does it imply a broader population or stronger design than the paper has?
- Is every subtitle term used in the paper?
- Can generic openings such as `On the`, `An analysis of`, or `New evidence on` be removed?

## Abstract architecture

Build a compact evidence chain, not a miniature introduction:

1. **Question/tension**: one sentence when the topic alone does not establish it.
2. **Approach**: data/setting/model and the source of leverage.
3. **Main result**: direction plus the most informative magnitude and uncertainty when available.
4. **Credibility or mechanism**: the single most decisive supporting fact.
5. **Interpretation/contribution**: what changes, at calibrated strength.

Not every abstract needs five sentences; each function must be recoverable. Put the main finding before a list of secondary analyses.

Build the abstract from the evidence ledger after the main exhibits are stable, but do not export the ledger into prose. Retain only evidence essential to recover the question, answer, credibility, central countervailing implication, or binding boundary. If a material consequence or trade-off changes how the main conclusion should be interpreted, give it prominence commensurate with that role rather than burying it in a generic qualification.

Do not turn `three findings`, a preferred sentence count, or any other number observed in an exemplar into a rule. Let salience and dependency determine how many findings or sentences survive.

Treat length and format as compliance-sensitive when the user also names a target journal. Verify the current official rule or a supplied live template. If the requested constraint conflicts with the verified rule, surface the conflict and obtain or state a deliberate resolution rather than silently following one constraint while claiming compliance. If the current rule cannot be verified, use `[CURRENT JOURNAL FORMAT RULE NEEDS VERIFICATION]` and do not infer it from published articles.

## Abstract modes

### Descriptive empirical

State the new fact, measurement, scope, benchmark, and interpretation. Do not imply a treatment effect.

### Causal empirical

Name the identifying event/variation, treated and comparison units when space permits, main estimate, design-defining validation, and scope.

### Asset pricing

Name the return/model object, construction or test, in-sample/out-of-sample distinction, economic magnitude, benchmark model, and implementability qualification when central.

### Pure theory

State the environment/friction, mechanism, equilibrium result, comparative static or welfare implication, and where the result depends on assumptions.

### Structural/quantitative

State the economic model, data moments/identification, fit or validation, counterfactual magnitude, and maintained-structure qualification.

## What to omit

- broad motivation that delays the result;
- unverified novelty claims;
- unexplained acronyms or variable names;
- a sequence of `we also` sentences;
- coefficient signs without units or economic interpretation;
- robustness laundry lists;
- policy prescriptions that outrun the design.

## Final abstract checks

- Can a reader identify the question, leverage, main answer, and contribution?
- Are population, period, and model scope honest?
- Do all numbers match the main tables?
- Does each retained quantity preserve the nearby unit, comparison, uncertainty, and evidentiary status needed to interpret it correctly?
- Is any material countervailing implication central to the answer visible at appropriate prominence?
- Does the abstract distinguish association, prediction, causality, and model implication?
- Would removing any sentence break the evidence chain? If not, compress it.
