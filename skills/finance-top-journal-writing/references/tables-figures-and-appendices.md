# Tables, Figures, Equations, and Appendices

## Tables

Give every table one evidentiary purpose. Make it interpretable without searching the prose:

- informative title stating the object, not the conclusion;
- defined sample and unit of observation;
- outcome, treatment/predictor, units, transformations, and timing;
- fixed effects, controls, weights, and inference method;
- exact definitions of significance markers;
- observation counts and fit statistics when meaningful;
- notes explaining nonstandard construction and exclusions.

Keep column order aligned with the narrative: baseline → design validation → preferred estimate → mechanism/heterogeneity. Do not hide a sample change between columns.

## Figures

Choose the visual for the claim:

- event-study/path plot for dynamics and pre-trends;
- binned/scatter plot for functional shape, with uncertainty and residualization disclosed;
- coefficient plot for comparable estimates;
- portfolio/factor plot for economic spread, benchmark, or cumulative performance;
- model-fit plot for targeted and untargeted moments;
- counterfactual plot for scenario comparison with baseline and units.

Label axes, units, sample, confidence intervals, and zero/reference lines. Do not use a visual that exaggerates magnitude through truncated axes or selective windows.

## Narrating exhibits

In prose, answer:

1. Why is this exhibit needed?
2. What comparison should the reader make?
3. What is the magnitude and uncertainty?
4. Which claim does it support?
5. What does it not establish?

Do not read every cell aloud.

## Equations

- Define each symbol at first use.
- State timing and information sets.
- Connect the equation to the economic object and empirical implementation.
- Use stable notation across text, tables, and appendix.
- Explain normalizations and identifying restrictions.
- Put routine derivations in the appendix; keep the economic intuition near the main result.

## Appendices and online material

Use an appendix map in the main paper. Put material in the appendix when it supports reproducibility or validation without carrying the core argument:

- detailed data construction and variable dictionary;
- additional institutional detail;
- proofs and derivations;
- auxiliary identification checks;
- alternative measures/specifications;
- extended model fit and counterfactual sensitivity;
- survey instruments, algorithms, and code/data notes.

Do not exile a fatal design issue or the only evidence for a central claim to the appendix. Cross-reference every appendix item from the main paper and keep numbering stable.
