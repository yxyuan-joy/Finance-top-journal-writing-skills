# JF, JFE, and RFS Journal Adapters

## Scope and precedence

Use these as observed production conventions from a full structural census of 2,065 strict ordinary-submission papers with canonical publication years 2020–2025, plus targeted PDF/Markdown checks. The census covers JF 452, JFE 896, and RFS 717 papers with 100% corresponding Markdown availability. A separate deliberately curated set informs rhetorical functions; it does not turn exemplar habits into journal rules. Apply this precedence:

1. User-supplied live template or accepted-paper format.
2. Current official journal instructions.
3. These recent published-paper conventions.
4. Generic finance style.

Never change facts, identification strength, or contribution merely to sound more “JF,” “JFE,” or “RFS.”

## Shared cross-journal architecture

Across the audited papers, the stable functional sequence is:

```text
title/authors/abstract
→ opening introduction
→ design- or model-led numbered body
→ results/validation/mechanism/extensions
→ concise conclusion
→ references/appendix/end matter
```

The introduction usually integrates the closest literature. In the full heading census, an explicit literature/related-work heading appeared in 83/452 JF, 169/896 JFE, and 111/717 RFS papers; OCR and heading semantics make these approximate structural indicators, not exact section inventories. Add a standalone literature section only when it performs real synthesis or theory-building work.

## JF adapter

Observed in the full JF census and direct checks:

- 452/452 had an explicit Abstract heading in the parsed structure.
- 0/452 had an explicit Introduction heading.
- Main sections used Roman numerals (`I.`, `II.`) and lettered subsections.
- End matter consistently included references and supporting-information signposting.

Practical adaptation:

- Do not add an `Introduction` heading when reproducing recent JF published structure unless a live template requires it.
- Use the opening pages to carry a relatively complete evidence chain; do not confuse length with contribution.
- Format headings only at the near-final stage. During drafting, semantic headings may remain for navigation and be removed later.

## JFE adapter

Observed in the full JFE census and direct checks:

- 862/896 (96.2%) had an explicit Introduction heading.
- Main sections used Arabic-decimal hierarchy (`1`, `2`, `2.1`, sometimes `2.1.1`).
- Publisher front matter often displayed article information, JEL classification, and an abstract block. MinerU sometimes spaces or fragments the Abstract label, so heading counts must not be used as abstract-presence counts.
- Recent end matter more often exposed separate data-availability, supplementary-material, CRediT, funding, or competing-interest modules.

Practical adaptation:

- Use explicit decimal headings when formatting a JFE-like near-final draft.
- Keep authorship, data, funding, and conflict statements factual; never fabricate them from the observed template.
- Treat published production metadata as publisher-added unless current author instructions say the author must supply it.

## RFS adapter

Observed in the full RFS census and direct checks:

- 0/717 had an explicit Abstract heading in the parsed structure.
- Only 14/717 (2.0%) had an explicit Introduction heading.
- The first numbered heading was often `1. Data`, `1. Model`, `1. Institutional Background`, or another substantive module.
- Main sections used Arabic-decimal hierarchy.
- Editorial-history or data/code notices sometimes appeared in front matter rather than under a terminal heading.

Practical adaptation:

- Do not insert visible Abstract/Introduction headings when matching recent RFS production unless the live template requires them.
- Begin numbering at the first post-introduction substantive section.
- Keep editorial-history language and decision dates out of an author draft unless officially required and verified.

## What the audit does not establish

- It does not establish journal preferences for a causal, theoretical, or stylistic “voice,” and it does not identify high-quality writing exemplars.
- It does not establish current word, abstract, figure, file, or appendix limits.
- It does not control fully for subfield composition.
- It does not convert published production layout into author-submission requirements.
- It does not imply that the independently curated portfolios contain the only good papers or that every section of an exemplar should be copied.

When a rule affects eligibility or submission compliance, verify the current official page rather than relying on this adapter.
