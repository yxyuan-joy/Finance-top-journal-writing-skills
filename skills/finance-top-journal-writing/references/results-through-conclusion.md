# Results, Robustness, Mechanisms, Discussion, and Conclusion

## Results paragraphs

Use this sequence when reporting an estimate:

1. State the estimand, comparison, or model object.
2. Give sign and magnitude with units.
3. Report uncertainty or model fit appropriate to the design.
4. Benchmark economic magnitude.
5. Interpret at the supported claim level.
6. Identify the table/figure and any material sample/specification difference.

Do not narrate every coefficient. Lead readers through the evidence needed for the paper's claims, including meaningful nulls and sign reversals.

At the section level, use an evidence-narrowing sequence when appropriate:

```text
baseline economic fact
→ design/model-specific credibility
→ evidence that separates competing accounts
→ scope, substitution, or equilibrium consequence
```

Each new exhibit should reduce a live uncertainty. If an exhibit changes no claim, threat assessment, interpretation, or boundary, move it to the appendix or remove it.

## Separate four evidentiary roles

### Mechanism

Tests a channel linking the main cause/object to the outcome. Stronger evidence uses timing, intermediate outcomes, cross-sectional predictions, or competing-channel tests derived before seeing results.

Write the mechanism as `channel → distinctive prediction → evidence → remaining alternatives`. Prefer evidence that rules in one implication while ruling out a genuinely competing account. A plausible story attached after the results is not a mechanism test.

### Heterogeneity

Shows effect/relationship differences across groups. It becomes mechanism evidence only when the split is theory-derived, measured before the outcome, and difficult to reconcile with alternatives.

### Mediation

Quantifies an intermediate pathway under additional assumptions. Do not call a coefficient reduction after adding a mediator proof of mediation.

### Alternative explanation

Assesses a competing account. A failed proxy test rarely eliminates the full alternative; state the residual ambiguity.

## Discussion

Use a discussion section when interpretation, equilibrium effects, external validity, or policy tradeoffs require synthesis beyond the results. Separate:

- what the evidence establishes;
- the preferred interpretation;
- plausible alternatives;
- scope and boundary conditions;
- implications conditional on those limits.

Do not turn the discussion into a second literature review.

## Conclusion

Use three compact moves:

1. Return to the economic question and answer.
2. Synthesize the decisive evidence and interpretation.
3. State implications and limitations or a precise future question.

Do not introduce new estimates, new mechanisms, new citations needed to support a claim, or a stronger causal statement than the results section. Avoid repeating the abstract sentence by sentence.
