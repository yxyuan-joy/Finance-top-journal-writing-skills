# Theory and Structural Section Blueprints

Use this workflow for pure theory, quantitative models, structural estimation, and theory–empirics hybrids. Route the paper, then complete applicable columns of the [primitives-to-welfare matrix](../assets/primitives-identification-fit-counterfactual-welfare-matrix.md). Pure theory needs no artificial identification or fit; an illustrative calibration is not a policy-ready structural exercise.

## Drafting sequence

1. State the economic tension and the minimal model feature needed to resolve it.
2. Separate primitives, endogenous objects, equilibrium closure, and outputs.
3. Link every major result to its assumptions and a transparent benchmark.
4. For quantitative work, classify inputs by source and moments as targeted or untargeted.
5. Draft counterfactual and welfare language only after fit on the policy-relevant margin is visible.
6. Apply the section gates below; unresolved matrix cells become bounded qualifications, not invented content.

## Abstract

**Function.** Present the economic puzzle, environment or friction, mechanism, decisive result, and implication without notation. For pure theory, state the proposition and conditions in economic language. For quantitative work, add the empirical discipline and quantitative result. For counterfactuals, state the intervention, benchmark, magnitude, and maintained-structure boundary.

**Required evidence.** Identify the agents and choice, what the benchmark lacks, and what becomes endogenous. Label each number as estimate, fit, decomposition, illustration, or counterfactual. Include material uncertainty or sensitivity.

**Common overclaims.** Do not call calibrated parameters identified; matching targeted moments validation; a model-consistent fact proof of the mechanism; an illustrative magnitude an estimated effect; or a counterfactual a causal experiment. Do not imply uniqueness if only existence or one equilibrium characterization is shown.

**Output gate.** A reader can say what economic force generates the result, under which conditions, and whether the implication is theorem-based, data-disciplined, or conditional on estimated structure. No symbol is required to understand the contribution.

## Introduction

**Function.** Build the paper's argument before the setup. Use paragraphs with distinct jobs:

1. Define an economic tension that existing mechanisms cannot resolve.
2. Introduce the minimal new friction, information structure, contract, or equilibrium closure.
3. Explain the mechanism in plain language as a sequence of incentives and responses.
4. State the central formal result and its economically meaningful conditions.
5. Show how the benchmark or nearest model behaves differently.
6. For hybrids, derive discriminating observable predictions.
7. For quantitative papers, preview data mapping, identification or calibration, fit, and headline counterfactual.
8. Position the contribution relative to the nearest models by changed primitive, equilibrium force, or quantitative discipline.
9. End with a domain or maintained-structure boundary when it changes interpretation.

**Required evidence.** Preview only results established later. Match empirical predictions to observables. Separate moments that discipline the model from those that assess it, and mechanism from quantitative importance.

**Common overclaims.** Complexity is not contribution. Ex-post rationalization is not a discriminating prediction. Better fit from greater flexibility need not validate the friction. An unprecedented ingredient combination is not itself a contribution.

**Output gate.** The model is motivated as necessary for the question, not as an end in itself. Readers know the benchmark, main result, source of discipline, and paper type before notation appears.

## Model setup

**Function.** Make the environment reproducible and reveal which assumptions do economic work. Recommended order: agents and environment; timeline; information; preferences/technology/contracts; constraints and feasible choices; strategies; market clearing or contracting protocol; equilibrium definition; benchmark or special case.

**Required evidence.** Separate primitives from endogenous objects. State domains, timing, observability, commitment, beliefs, equilibrium selection, and resource constraints. Link central assumptions to results; separate normalizations from restrictions; maintain one symbol per object.

**Common overclaims.** Verbal assumptions may hide functional-form or selection restrictions. Normalizations cannot supply empirical content. Primitives cannot silently change across comparative statics. Do not omit beliefs or boundary conditions that select the result.

**Output gate.** A technically trained reader could reconstruct the game or economy and distinguish primitives, choices, equilibrium conditions, and solution objects. Removing the central friction yields an explicit benchmark.

## Formal results and mechanism

**Function.** Turn mathematics into cumulative economic understanding. For every proposition or theorem use: precise statement and conditions → one-sentence economic content → mechanism → benchmark comparison → comparative-static, empirical, or normative implication → proof location. Separate existence, uniqueness, characterization, efficiency, and implementation results.

**Required evidence.** Match conditions to proofs and equations. Explain the incentive, constraint, or feedback driving the result. Use counterexamples, limits, or illustrations when useful, without presenting illustration as proof. Trace the endogenous sequence.

**Common overclaims.** A sufficient condition is not necessary; local comparative statics are not global; one selected equilibrium is not uniqueness; private optimality is not social efficiency; and a sign result need not imply quantitative importance.

**Output gate.** Every result has economic intuition not reducible to restating its sign, every proof reference resolves, and the text never claims beyond the proposition's domain. The sequence of results answers the research question rather than cataloging derivations.

## Data, identification, and estimation

**Function.** For structural and quantitative papers, show how observables discipline latent objects and parameters. For hybrids, explain the mapping used by each test. Omit this module for pure theory without data.

**Required evidence.** Define sample, measurement, and model-to-data mapping. Record each parameter's source, disciplining variation or moments, observational equivalence, and uncertainty. Explain objective, weighting, computation, uncertainty, and convergence. Prelabel targeted moments and the relevant identification concept.

**Common overclaims.** A unique numerical optimum need not imply economic identification. Functional form, equilibrium selection, auxiliary measurement, or normalization may supply separation. Precise standard errors conditional on the model do not absorb misspecification uncertainty. Reusing a target as validation double-counts evidence.

**Output gate.** Each policy-relevant parameter has an auditable source of discipline, every normalization is visible, and readers can tell what the data identify versus what maintained structure supplies.

## Fit and validation

**Function.** Establish that the model reproduces the margins used for interpretation and especially those a counterfactual moves. Order evidence as targeted fit; untargeted or holdout fit; comparison with simpler benchmarks; known misses; parameter and assumption sensitivity.

**Required evidence.** Report moment definitions, weights, units, and uncertainty. Separate in-sample from external validation. For hybrids, name the competing mechanism and its prediction. Show fit on margins driving policy conclusions.

**Common overclaims.** Average fit can hide failure on the decisive margin. Post-selected untargeted moments are not holdout evidence. More moments do not cure weak identification. Qualitative consistency does not validate magnitude.

**Output gate.** Readers can see what was matched by construction, what was independently tested, where the model fails, and whether the evidence reaches the mechanism used in the next section.

## Counterfactuals and welfare

**Function.** Translate a disciplined model into conditional intervention analysis. Define baseline and policy change; invariant primitives; direct behavioral response; equilibrium adjustment and closure; transition; aggregate and distributional outcomes; uncertainty; and sensitivity. For welfare, define agents, objective, transfers, resources, externalities, weights, information, commitment, and implementation.

**Required evidence.** Explain whether the experiment changes a primitive, constraint, rule, or equilibrium selection. Identify extrapolation beyond observed support. Decompose direct, indirect, price, selection, substitution, and general-equilibrium effects where relevant. Report winners and losers separately from aggregate welfare. When group components are percentage changes, state each denominator and the aggregation formula; never add percentages with different or undisclosed bases. Compare with a feasible benchmark policy, not only no intervention.

**Common overclaims.** A decomposition is not an intervention. Invariance of preferences, technology, or policy rules is an assumption, not a result. Transfers are not resource costs unless modeled as such. More output, liquidity, credit, stability, or surplus for one group is not automatically higher social welfare. Steady-state gains can ignore transition losses.

**Output gate.** The counterfactual is reproducible from stated changes and closure, the result is accompanied by policy-relevant fit and sensitivity, and welfare conclusions identify who gains, who loses, and which maintained assumptions can reverse the ranking.

## Conclusion

**Function.** Restate the mechanism and central formal or quantitative result, then give the conditional economic implication and the most important boundary. Do not summarize the table of contents.

**Required evidence.** Preserve the proposition domain, population, calibration, and counterfactual benchmark used earlier. Label empirical facts, model implications, and welfare conclusions distinctly.

**Common overclaims.** Do not upgrade possibility to necessity, local to universal, calibrated to identified, fit to truth, or conditional welfare to unconditional policy superiority. Introduce no new extension or result.

**Output gate.** Every claim appeared with support earlier, the model's role remains explicit, and the final sentence stays correct after adding the most important assumption or extrapolation qualifier.

## Whole-paper output gate

Confirm that primitive definitions, equilibrium concept, proposition conditions, parameter status, moment labels, counterfactual baseline, and welfare accounting remain consistent across abstract, introduction, body, tables, and conclusion. Every central claim must trace through the matrix from primitive to result and—only where applicable—identification, fit, counterfactual, and welfare.
