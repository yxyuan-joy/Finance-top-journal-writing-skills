# Theory and Structural Section Blueprints

## Abstract

Puzzle → environment/friction → mechanism/model discipline → central proposition or quantitative result → comparative-static, counterfactual, or welfare implication with boundary.

## Introduction

1. Economic tension existing models/evidence cannot resolve.
2. Minimal description of the new environment/friction.
3. Mechanism in plain language.
4. Main formal result and conditions.
5. Empirical predictions or model-to-data mapping.
6. Quantitative fit/counterfactual when relevant.
7. Exact relationship to the nearest models.
8. Assumption/domain boundary.

## Model setup

Recommended order:

1. Environment and agents.
2. Timeline and information.
3. Preferences/technology/contracts/constraints.
4. Strategies/choices.
5. Equilibrium definition.
6. Benchmark/special case.

## Theory results

For each formal result:

- state conditions precisely;
- give one-sentence economic content;
- explain the force behind the result;
- compare with benchmark;
- state empirical or welfare relevance;
- locate proof.

## Quantitative/structural order

1. Data and observable mapping.
2. Identification/calibration.
3. Estimation/computation.
4. Parameter estimates and uncertainty.
5. In-sample fit.
6. Untargeted/external validation.
7. Mechanism decomposition.
8. Counterfactual/welfare.
9. Sensitivity and limitations.

## Conclusion

Restate mechanism and central result, then the conditional implication. Do not claim empirical truth for assumptions not tested or universal policy superiority from a narrow counterfactual.
