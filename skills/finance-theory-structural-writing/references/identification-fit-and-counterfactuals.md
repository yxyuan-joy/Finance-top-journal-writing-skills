## Identification writing

- Explain identification in economic language before formal rank/order conditions.
- Distinguish variation that moves one primitive from variation affecting several.
- State normalizations and observational equivalence.
- Separate point, set, local, and functional-form identification.
- Explain dependence on auxiliary measurement and equilibrium selection.

## Fit

Report:

- targeted moments and weights;
- untargeted moments;
- within-sample fit and meaningful external/holdout validation;
- dimensions where the model misses;
- parameter and sampling uncertainty;
- fit relative to simpler benchmarks.

Good average fit does not validate the mechanism most important for a counterfactual. Show fit on the margin the policy changes.

## Counterfactual checklist

1. Define policy/environment change and baseline.
2. State which primitives remain invariant.
3. Explain equilibrium adjustment and closure.
4. Report direct and indirect effects.
5. Quantify uncertainty/sensitivity.
6. Distinguish decomposition from intervention.
7. State whether the counterfactual leaves observed support.
8. Identify implementation and transition assumptions.

## Welfare

Define welfare function, affected agents, transfers, externalities, distributional weights, planner information, commitment, and transition. Report winners/losers and decomposition. Avoid treating producer surplus, consumer surplus, liquidity, output, or stability as the entire welfare criterion unless the model explicitly does so.
