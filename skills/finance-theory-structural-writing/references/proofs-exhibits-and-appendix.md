# Proofs, Exhibits, and Appendix

## Proof presentation

- Keep theorem/proposition statement and all conditions together.
- Follow with economic intuition and the binding force.
- State whether the result is necessary, sufficient, local, global, generic, or numerical.
- Use lemmas only when they clarify logic or support reuse.
- Put long algebra in the appendix and give a proof roadmap in the main text.
- Keep notation stable and provide an index when extensive.
- Verify cross-references and special cases.

## Exhibits

- Timeline/decision tree for complex timing or information.
- Equilibrium diagram for regions, multiplicity, or comparative statics.
- Fit table separating targeted and untargeted moments.
- Parameter table with meaning, source, estimate/calibration, and uncertainty.
- Mechanism decomposition with a clearly defined baseline.
- Counterfactual plot with units, equilibrium response, uncertainty, and sensitivity.

Do not use simulations as proof of a general proposition. Label numerical examples and parameter choices.

## Appendix structure

Suggested order:

1. Proofs in main-text order.
2. Additional model characterization.
3. Data and observable mapping.
4. Identification and estimation detail.
5. Computational algorithm/convergence.
6. Additional fit and validation.
7. Counterfactual and welfare sensitivity.
8. Alternative assumptions/equilibrium selection.

Cross-reference every appendix item. Keep the main text sufficient to understand the mechanism and credibility without reading all derivations.
