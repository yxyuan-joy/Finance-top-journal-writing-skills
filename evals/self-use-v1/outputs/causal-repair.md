# Prioritized audit

> SYNTHETIC SELF-USE OUTPUT: all manuscript facts below were invented for this test.

1. **Critical — treatment timing and parallel-trends claim.** The event-time −2 estimate is +1.1 percentage points with a confidence interval excluding zero. Announcement occurs about nine months before implementation, but the calendar span of the −2 bin is not supplied. The lead cannot be attributed to anticipation unless that bin overlaps the documented announcement window; on the current record, it is adverse pre-treatment movement with unresolved timing. The manuscript cannot state that parallel trends “is satisfied.”
2. **Critical — outcome is not credit supply.** The reported outcome is application approval probability. A 2.4-percentage-point approval change cannot be relabeled as credit supply without evidence on amounts, prices, lender willingness conditional on demand, or borrower substitution. Application volume changes after announcement, making demand and composition especially material.
3. **High — current TWFE uses inadmissible comparisons.** Already-treated states enter the current TWFE control group despite staggered timing. The cohort-time estimate using not-yet-treated controls better matches the stated ATT, but its causal interpretation remains conditional on the unresolved announcement/pretrend issue.
4. **High — changing applicant pool.** Applications rise 4% after announcement. Stable observed applicant characteristics do not rule out treatment-induced selection on unobservables, so the approval estimand may partly reflect composition changes.
5. **High — spillovers and inference.** No bordering-state spillover analysis is supplied. State clustering matches treatment assignment, but 32 state clusters warrant transparent finite-cluster sensitivity rather than automatic confidence in conventional inference.

# Rewritten design and results passage

We target the average effect of registry adoption on approval probability for loan applicants in adopting states during the first two post-implementation years. Because adoption is staggered from 2012 through 2019, our preferred cohort-time design compares each treated cohort only with states that have not yet adopted at the same date; already-treated states do not serve as controls. We aggregate these cohort-time contrasts over the stated two-year horizon. The identifying assumption is that, absent registry adoption and announcement-related responses, approval probabilities for applicants in adopting states would have followed those of the eligible not-yet-treated states.

The preferred estimate is a 2.4-percentage-point increase in approval probability (95% confidence interval: 0.5 to 4.3 percentage points), relative to a 36% baseline approval rate. This corresponds to approximately 6.7% of the baseline mean. The estimate is an ATT for applicants observed in adopting states over the specified horizon; it is neither an effect for all states and years nor direct evidence on the quantity of credit supplied.

The event-time evidence limits a causal interpretation under the current timing definition. At event time −2, approval probability is already 1.1 percentage points higher (95% confidence interval: 0.2 to 2.0). The registry was publicly announced roughly nine months before implementation, but the task does not map the −2 bin into calendar dates. The announcement cannot explain this lead unless the bin actually overlaps that nine-month window. Until that mapping is shown, the estimate is adverse pre-treatment movement, and an anticipation label is unsupported. Application volume also rises 4% after announcement. Although observed applicant characteristics remain stable, unobserved composition may change, meaning that the post-adoption approval contrast need not hold the applicant pool fixed. The manuscript should report calendar-time support, define any anticipation treatment from the institutional timeline, and reassess the cohort-time estimate under that definition. Border-state spillovers and inference sensitivity with 32 state clusters also remain unresolved.

# Strongest currently defensible headline

Using not-yet-treated states as controls, registry adoption is associated with a 2.4-percentage-point increase in applicant approval during the first two implementation years, but announcement-period movement, changing application volume, and untested spillovers currently prevent an unqualified causal or credit-supply interpretation.
