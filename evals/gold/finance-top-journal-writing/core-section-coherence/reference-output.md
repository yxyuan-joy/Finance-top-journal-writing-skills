## Revised abstract

We examine whether supplier concentration is associated with corporate cash holdings. Using 18,420 U.S. public-firm-year observations from 2004 through 2022, we estimate descriptive firm-panel regressions that relate supplier concentration to cash as a share of assets. A one-standard-deviation increase in supplier concentration is associated with a 0.8 percentage-point increase in cash/assets (95% confidence interval: 0.3 to 1.3 percentage points). Relative to the sample mean of 12.4%, this estimate represents an increase of approximately 6.5% of mean cash holdings. The association is larger among firms without a credit rating, a pattern consistent with—but not sufficient to establish—a financing-risk channel. Because the design does not isolate quasi-experimental variation and the split by rating status was not preregistered, we interpret the estimates as descriptive evidence rather than causal effects or definitive mechanism tests. The findings document where the concentration–cash association is strongest while leaving its causal source and applicability beyond the sample unresolved.
